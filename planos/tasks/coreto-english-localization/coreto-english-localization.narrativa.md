# Narrative contract

Owns RQ-001/003/005/006 in [spec.md](spec.md). The implementing agent translates
all in-scope copy; Edney personally accepts its editorial quality. Stage 1 and
the technical design are approved (D-008, D-015).

Use the current native game as the extraction baseline and the canonical GDD
for meaning. Preserve source provenance and requirement status; do not resolve
old creative gaps or silently rewrite Portuguese. Each key records scene and
speaker/outcome context, so the translator can distinguish identical words with
different functions. The table is the single prose owner (ADR-002).

Use natural American English, familiar vocabulary and clear referents. Retain
horror through image, rhythm, uncertainty and consequence rather than ornate
synonyms. Preserve clues, intentional ambiguity, character voice, names and
information. Maintain accents in character/folklore names. Translate surrounding
descriptive words consistently using a reviewed glossary. Preserve the approved
English game title. No invented lore, explanatory additions or abridged outcomes.

Review the entire corpus, including all branches, menus and notices. Repeated
choice representations share meaning and key. Preserve all interpolation and
control tokens. Dialogue length may change but cannot silently merge speaking
turns, skip acknowledgements or change commitment boundaries.

The separate-agent evaluation has two portions: a genuine observed browser
journey through normal inputs, then a read-only corpus review for unvisited
branches. It identifies concrete comprehension problems and atmosphere loss.
Its coverage and limitations remain explicit; an agent persona is not a human
playtest. No opaque reading-level score replaces evidence.

Findings identify key, source context, quote, difficulty, narrative effect and
suggested direction. The translator addresses them; Edney decides editorial
disposition. Technical success or review report creation is not final acceptance.
See [verification](verification.md), especially V-004/V-005.
