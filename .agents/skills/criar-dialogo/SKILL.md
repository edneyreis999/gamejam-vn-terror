# Create dialogue

## 1. Understand the scene

Read the character sheets and the request. Answer the questions below internally; ask the requester only for information still needed to decide the scene. Use concrete events and actions. Accept “none” where appropriate.

- Where are they, and what just happened to start the conversation?
- What does each want from the other right now? What makes that difficult?
- What does each know, hide, or avoid saying? What does the reader know that they do not?
- What state is each in as the conversation begins? Which earlier events need to show in their reactions?
- How does each try to get what they want? What do they do if that fails?
- Who leads the conversation? Does that change?
- How does the scene end? What has changed between them, even if nothing changes outwardly?
- Which events are fixed, and what may be invented?

These questions support creation: let writing choices emerge; confirm facts that depend on the requester.

Finish when the gaps that would change events or characters have been answered.

## 2. Write and revise

Use the character sheets to compose lines suited to the other speaker and the moment in the scene. Write with freedom of rhythm, silence, interruption, and sentence construction. Let each character react to what the other does and says.

Let each voice determine when to be brief and when to develop an idea. Invent within the agreed latitude, preserving obstacles as well; consult the requester about continuity changes beyond it.

Reread the scene: are the subjects understandable, are the voices distinct, and does each exchange add something? Cut repetition that has no effect and rewrite what fails.

Finish by delivering the dialogue, with whatever narration the scene calls for, without printing the internal planning.
