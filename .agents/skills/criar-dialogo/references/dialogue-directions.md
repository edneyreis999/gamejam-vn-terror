# Dialogue directions

## Scene purpose and scope

Define what the encounter needs to give the player: information, feeling, a relationship experience, a basis for a choice, or a combination. An introduction can establish a motive and practical contribution; ordinary company and grief call for different exchanges.

Keep contracted participants, revelations, consequences, and entry conditions. With an optional speaker or suffix, make the base complete without it and let the addition respond to what is available in that branch.

Let the ending follow the purpose. Acknowledgment, an unanswered question, or continued company can suffice; a change of control or relationship is an available dramatic turn, not a requirement.

## Situated intention and response

Let each participant's immediate intention select which aspects of their personality emerge. Use the sheet as a repertoire of tendencies, not a sequence to perform. Someone may seek to persuade, console, hide something, provoke, or share company. Decide how they address this particular listener to pursue that intention, and let what they hear affect their next move.

Give first contact a social opening suited to familiarity and circumstance; urgency or an ongoing exchange can justify starting elsewhere. Respect obligations and authority: asking permission, offering help, issuing an order, and expressing concern carry different expectations. A helpful character can volunteer without treating everyone as a superior.

Build questions from the speaker's interest and what they have heard. Track what has been asked, offered, accepted, refused, or evaded. Make rebukes, confessions, praise, and changes of attitude proportionate to a cause the player can perceive. Private biography can motivate a response, but an explanation outside the scene cannot supply a missing conversational cause.

Read a statement's meaning in the interaction before treating it as objective information or a mechanical guarantee. It may convey confidence, exaggeration, humor, or an attempt to impress. Distinguish a character's claim from an established fact, while respecting explicit truthfulness contracts and the game's actual limits. Practical contribution should remain grounded in believable experience.

## Speakability and information

Write thoughts this character could plausibly say to this listener. Preserve the connections needed to follow them, including contractions, conjunctions, and implied subjects where natural in the language. Let vocabulary, sentence length, humor, profanity, and rhythm vary with intention, relationship, and circumstance.

Select information for its use in the exchange or the player's understanding. A detail in the sheet need not become spoken exposition.

Give enough context for the current stakes. Names and specific terms can be useful without a full explanation; add a relationship label only when supported or within the creative latitude. A concise character chooses to say little while remaining understandable. A longer turn may develop a justification across several sentences. Organize turn-taking around opportunities to respond: a question, offer, or request may need the listener's reaction before the subject moves on. Use punctuation to guide the thought and rhythm within the turn.

## Dialogue boxes and performance

Place box breaks, pauses, and emphasis where they help the player perceive a change of action, intention, or emotional pressure. Follow the beat and the medium's limits rather than a sentence quota.

Use available staging or narration for physical actions when needed, leaving speech to perform the invitation, warning, offer, or response. Essential meaning must survive unavailable prop animation, unseen acting, or disabled effects. Keep production directions separate from spoken text and narration according to the medium's contract.

## Knowledge and continuity

Distinguish what speakers know, how they learned it, and what the player has been shown. When the source of knowledge matters, establish it economically through an introduction, document, shared task, or another supported connection.

Choose connections to established behavior, experience, or aspirations because they serve this encounter. For optional prior scenes, make the present exchange understandable on its own while allowing returning players something additional to recognize. Refer to a specific shared incident only when the branch establishes it; otherwise preserve uncertainty about routes, witnesses, and timing.

## Invention and adoption

Create small connecting facts within the agreed latitude, preserving existing obstacles. Resolve routine details through judgment; consult the requester for contradictions with established facts or material plot, participant, revelation, or gameplay changes beyond that latitude.

Distinguish a draft proposal, an adopted continuity fact, and a source-derived fact. Before subsequent authors rely on a new adopted fact, record its content, scope, status, and authored origin in the existing authority: the GDD for world facts or shared rules, a character sheet for personal history or relationships, or the appropriate narrative document. If the current role cannot edit that document, hand the fact to its authorized owner with the pending record explicit.

Persist details that establish continuity. Social phrasing, metaphors, incidental suggestions, and hypothetical exercises do not automatically become canon or prove an event occurred. Use the existing authorities rather than a parallel archive of dialogue.

## Reread and revise

First read the whole conversation in spoken order, with only the player's guaranteed prior context and available staging, before considering the writer's defense or annotations. Check whether the people sound plausible with one another, replies respond to what was said, references are intelligible, and the intended relationship or feeling emerges. Assigning a logical function to every sentence is insufficient. Judge greetings, brevity, repetition, connectors, and silence by their effect in this reading.

Then consult the brief and sources to check purpose, voice, knowledge, and continuity limits. Read each eligible branch on its own and check how optional additions connect to the base.

For a material failure, identify the passage, its effect on the player's reading, and the scope that needs repair. Reconstruct the exchange when isolated word substitutions cannot fix the interaction; preserve effective passages rather than revising for stylistic preference alone.

After repairs, reread the entire conversation, including previously accepted passages, and repeat the source checks affected by the changes. Closing individual findings does not establish that the interaction now works. Finish when no material failure remains, or report the unresolved conflict explicitly. An editorial PASS or technical validation remains distinct from human acceptance and emotional response.
