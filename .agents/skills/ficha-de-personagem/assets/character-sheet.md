## 3. Written speech signature

Describe patterns that can guide writing a new line. A catchphrase can reinforce identity, but should not be its only marker.

| Aspect | Writing direction |
| --- | --- |
| Predominant tone | [What attitude do they convey? How do the words make it perceptible?] |
| Vocabulary | [Which words do they prefer? Do they use concrete language, technical terms, colloquial expressions?] |
| Formality | [How do they construct a casual conversation? When do they become more or less formal?] |
| Addressing others | [How do they address someone, refer to themselves, and establish closeness or distance?] |
| Sentence length | [Do they tend toward short responses or develop explanations? When does this change?] |
| Sentence construction | [Do they get to the point, justify before answering, ask questions, correct their own reasoning?] |
| Reading rhythm | [How do they use pauses, interruptions, repetitions, and hesitations? In what situation does each appear?] |
| Humor | [How do they joke or react to other people's humor? They may not joke.] |
| Comparisons and references | [What experiences or subjects do they draw on to explain the world?] |
| Verbal mannerisms | [What habits appear in how they start, develop, or end a line? How often and in what situation?] |
| Characteristic expressions | [Which do they use, how often, and in what situation? They may have no catchphrase.] |
| What to avoid | [Which words, constructions, and habits would sound strange for this character?] |
